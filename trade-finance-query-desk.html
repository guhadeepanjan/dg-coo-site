    Documentary credits

    The goods were shipped in time, but documents reached us 25 days after shipment. Refuse?

    Yes — a presentation that includes an original transport document must be made no later than 21 calendar days after the date of shipment, and in any event by the expiry date (UCP 600 Art. 14(c)). Day 25 is late presentation, even though shipment itself was timely. Refuse under Art. 16, or seek the applicant’s waiver — the choice is the bank’s, not the applicant’s to impose.

    The invoice describes the goods slightly differently from the credit. Is that fatal?

    On the invoice, yes, it can be: the description of the goods in the commercial invoice must correspond with the description in the credit (Art. 18(c)). Other documents may show the goods in general terms not conflicting with the credit (Art. 14(e)). Same word order is not required — but a different product, grade or specification on the invoice is a discrepancy.

    We found discrepancies on a Friday. When must our refusal notice be sent?

    By close of business on the fifth banking day following the day of presentation (Art. 14(b) and Art. 16(d)). One single notice, stating every discrepancy and how the documents are being held (Art. 16(c)). Miss the deadline or the content, and the bank is precluded from claiming the documents are discrepant (Art. 16(f)) — it must honour.

    Standbys and guarantees

    A complying demand under our guarantee arrived two days after expiry. Do we pay?

    No. Under URDG 758 a demand must be presented at the place of issue on or before expiry (Art. 14 and Art. 25); a demand after expiry is not a complying demand, however genuine the underlying default. The guarantee has expired — the beneficiary’s remedy lies against the applicant, not the guarantor.

    The beneficiary says “extend or pay”. What are our options and how long do we have?

    Under URDG 758 Art. 23 the guarantor may suspend payment for up to 30 calendar days while it seeks the applicant’s agreement to extend. If the guarantee is extended, the demand falls away; if not, the guarantor must pay the demand within that window. Under an ISP98 standby the same idea appears in the extend-or-pay rules — the issuer may treat the demand as a demand for payment if it does not extend.

    The demand is in the right form but there is no statement that the applicant is in breach. Complying?

    Under URDG 758, no: a demand must be supported by a statement indicating in what respect the applicant is in breach of the underlying relationship (Art. 15(a)), unless the guarantee itself excludes it. Under ISP98 a standby may require no statement at all — it depends on what the standby calls for. Read the instrument first; the rules fill gaps, they do not rewrite it.

    Collections, transfers and revolving credits

    Under a D/P collection the buyer asks us to release the documents against his trust receipt. Can we?

    Only if the collection instruction authorises it. Under URC 522 the collecting bank releases documents strictly on the terms of the collection instruction; documents against payment means against payment. Releasing against a trust receipt without authority is the bank’s own risk — if the buyer then defaults, the bank answers to the remitting bank.

    Can a transferable credit be transferred a second time?

    No. A second beneficiary cannot transfer onward (UCP 600 Art. 38(d)) — the exception is a transfer back to the first beneficiary. The first beneficiary may substitute its own invoices and drafts for the second beneficiary’s, within the limits and mechanics of Art. 38, and keeps the difference as its margin.

    Our revolving credit is “cumulative”. What does that change?

    Any amount not drawn in one period carries forward and can be drawn in later periods, on top of that period’s amount — total exposure grows if drawings bunch up. In a non-cumulative credit, an undrawn amount simply lapses. Either way, control the aggregate: the credit should state an overall cap, and reinstatement after a drawing happens only on the terms the credit sets out.

    We advanced money under a red clause and the beneficiary never shipped. Who repays us?

    The advance is made at the issuing bank’s risk under the clause: a bank that advances in good faith against the beneficiary’s receipt and undertaking is entitled to reimbursement from the issuing bank even if the goods are never shipped, and the issuing bank looks to its applicant. That protection is exactly why the clause’s wording — amount, documents, undertaking — must be followed to the letter.

    How this Desk works

    Sources. Answers are generated automatically from summaries of UCP 600 and ISBP 821 (documentary credits), ISP98 (standbys), URC 522 (collections), URDG 758 (demand guarantees), eUCP v2.1 (electronic presentation) and URR 725 (reimbursements), together with the CDCS study text (2021 edition) and the CTFC trade-based financial crime compliance text. Reference material last reviewed: November 2026.

    Known limits, stated plainly. The UCP 600 and ISBP material is detailed; the ISP98 and URR 725 material is held at summary level, so answers resting on those rules carry a note to check the official text. The Desk gives the ICC position only — it does not cover local law or a local regulator’s practice, which can change the outcome where you sit. Topics outside the eight products above — forfaiting, Islamic trade finance structures, supply-chain finance — are outside the Desk’s reference material; the Desk will say so rather than improvise.

    Check the rules yourself. The official texts are published by the ICC — see the ICC Academy and ICC websites. Where an answer turns on a pinpoint sub-paragraph the Desk could not verify, it cites the article alone rather than guess.

    Common questions

    Is the Trade Finance Query Desk free?

    Yes. It is free to use, with no sign-up and no OTP. To keep it free and fair for everyone, questions are limited to 10 a day per visitor.

    Are my questions stored?

    No. This website does not store your questions. Please do not type anything sensitive — no customer names, account numbers or confidential deal details.

    Which rules does the Desk cover?

    UCP 600 and ISBP 821 for documentary credits, ISP98 for standbys, URC 522 for collections, URDG 758 for demand guarantees, eUCP v2.1 for electronic presentation, and the CDCS and CTFC study material behind them.

    Can I rely on an answer for a live transaction?

    Treat every answer as guidance, not as an official ICC opinion or legal advice. Check critical points against the official rule texts before you act on a live transaction.
